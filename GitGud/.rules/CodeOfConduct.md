# Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in this project: maintainers, team members, external contributors, reviewers, users, and anyone who interacts with the project in any space.

Related documents: [Contributing.md](Contributing.md), [CodeReview.md](CodeReview.md), [Security.md](Security.md), [Agent.md](Agent.md).

---

## 1. Our Pledge

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

---

## 2. Our Global Community

Our members come from all over the world. We have different languages, cultures, time zones, working styles, and customs. We expect everyone to take this into account.

- **Language:** English is the working language of the project, and for many of us it is not a first language. Be patient with spelling, grammar, and phrasing. Do not mock or correct people in a way that embarrasses them. Ask for clarification instead of assuming a bad meaning.
- **Write clearly:** use plain language. Avoid slang, idioms, sarcasm, and cultural references that may not translate. Humor often does not cross cultures, so use it carefully.
- **Cultural differences:** communication styles differ. Some cultures are direct, others indirect. Silence, directness, and disagreement carry different meanings. Do not assume rudeness or incompetence from a different style.
- **Time zones and availability:** do not expect immediate answers. Respect working hours, holidays, and religious or cultural observances. Set deadlines with this in mind.
- **Names and identity:** use the name and pronouns a person gives you. Do not guess, shorten, or anglicize names without permission.
- **Different backgrounds and experience:** assume good intent and limited context. Explain without condescension. Every contributor was a beginner once.
- **Accessibility:** write documentation and communication in a way that is accessible to all, for example with alt text for images and readable formatting.

## 3. Expected Behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Be respectful, polite, and professional to everyone.
- Treat people with dignity, regardless of role or seniority.
- Demonstrate empathy and kindness toward others.
- Be open to different opinions, viewpoints, and experiences.
- Give and accept constructive feedback gracefully. Critique the work, never the person (see [CodeReview.md](CodeReview.md) Section 8.3).
- Assume good intent. Ask questions before drawing conclusions.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, apologize to those affected, and learn from them.
- Focus on what is best for the community and the project, not only for the individual.
- Credit others for their work and ideas.
- Respect the time and effort of volunteers and maintainers.
- Help newcomers and welcome questions.
- Respect privacy and confidentiality.

---

## 4. Unacceptable Behavior

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Harassment of any kind: offensive comments, insults, slurs, derogatory remarks, or demeaning jokes, including about the characteristics listed in Section 1.
- Discrimination or stereotyping of individuals or groups.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment, stalking, or intimidation.
- Threats or incitement of violence, or encouraging self-harm.
- Publishing others' private information (such as a physical address, email address, or other personal data) without explicit permission (doxxing).
- Deliberately misgendering a person or using a name they asked you not to use.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, repeated unwelcome contact, or deliberate intimidation.
- Mocking someone's language skills, accent, or background.
- Sharing content that is illegal, or that infringes copyright or licenses.
- Submitting deceptive, malicious, or intentionally harmful contributions, or abusing the project's tools and processes.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem or takes part in an investigation.
- Other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

Disagreement is welcome. Disrespect is not. Strong technical arguments are fine when expressed respectfully.

---

## 5. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces and when an individual officially represents the project in public spaces. This includes, without limitation:

- Repositories: code, commits, issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and documentation.
- Chat, mailing lists, forums, and video calls.
- Events, meetups, and conferences organized by or for the project.
- Social media accounts that represent the project.
- Private communication that results from project participation.
- Conduct outside these spaces that affects the safety or well-being of community members, when it is connected to the project.

It also applies to **AI-generated content** submitted by members. The human submitting it is responsible for its content and tone (see [Agent.md](Agent.md)).

## 6. Responsibilities of Maintainers

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing the standards of acceptable behavior and will take fair and appropriate corrective action in response to any behavior they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that do not align with this Code of Conduct, and will communicate the reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

Maintainers:
- Lead by example.
- Treat all reports fairly, seriously, and without bias.
- Respect the privacy and safety of the reporter.
- Recuse themselves from cases where they have a conflict of interest.
- Keep records of reports and decisions, stored securely.

## 7. Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, or have any other concern, please report it.

### 7.1 How to report
- Email: `<conduct-contact@example.com>` (replace with the real address, monitored by more than one person).
- Alternatively, contact any maintainer privately.
- If the report involves a maintainer, contact another maintainer or the escalation contact: `<escalation-contact@example.com>`.
- Do **not** report in public issues or pull requests. Use a private channel.
- Security vulnerabilities are not conduct reports. Follow [Security.md](Security.md) Section 16.

### 7.2 What to include
- Your contact information (anonymous reports are accepted, but may limit what we can do).
- Names or handles of those involved.
- What happened, when, and where, with links or screenshots if possible.
- Any other relevant context.

### 7.3 Our commitments
- All reports are reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.
- We acknowledge a report within **3 business days**.
- Reports are handled with **confidentiality**. Information is shared only with those who need it to resolve the matter.
- We do not tolerate retaliation against reporters or witnesses.
- We aim to give the reporter an update on the outcome where it is appropriate and safe to do so.
- Reports made in good faith are never penalized, even if the conclusion is that no violation occurred.

## 8. Enforcement Guidelines

Maintainers follow these guidelines when deciding the consequences of a violation. Decisions consider the severity, context, intent, impact, and history.

| Level | Behavior | Consequence |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **1. Correction** | Inappropriate language or behavior judged unprofessional or unwelcome | A private, written warning with clarity about the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested. |
| **2. Warning** | A violation through a single incident or a series of actions | A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited contact with those enforcing the Code, for a specified period. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban. |
| **3. Temporary ban** | A serious violation, or sustained inappropriate behavior | A temporary ban from any interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period. No public or private interaction with the people involved during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban. |
| **4. Permanent ban** | A pattern of violations, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of groups | A permanent ban from all interaction within the community. |

Immediate action, such as removing content or temporarily suspending access, may be taken while a report is reviewed, when it is needed to protect people.

### 8.1 Process
1. A report is received and acknowledged.
2. At least two maintainers, not involved in the matter, review it.
3. The people involved may be asked for their account. Everyone is heard.
4. A decision and consequence are agreed on, in line with Section 8.
5. The affected people are informed. Records are kept confidentially.

### 8.2 Appeals
A person who received a consequence may appeal to the escalation contact within 14 days, with a short explanation. The appeal is reviewed by maintainers who were not involved in the original decision. Their decision is final.

## 9. Consequences in the Project

Consequences may include removal of access, rejection or reversal of contributions, removal from CODEOWNERS and teams, and loss of permissions to repositories and CI. Access of the person is revoked in line with [Security.md](Security.md) and [BuildAndCI.md](BuildAndCI.md) where required.
